Three ideas for a new homepage

Mark, I took a look at your website and put together three ideas for what a new MD Restoration homepage could look like. Each one is built from your own seal, your own photos and the headings already on your site, so you can react to a real design instead of a description.

These are just ideas to react to. Most people like the feel of one, a section from another and the color from a third, and that's completely fine. If something catches your eye, we'd love to hear what pulls you in and what doesn't.

Read this first

These are design comps, not a working website. Each one stops after the services section and jumps to the footer, because the point right now is the look and feel rather than the whole page. The paragraphs are placeholder Latin on purpose, since writing the real words is its own step. Nothing on these pages clicks, including the call and text buttons.

Direction one

Round the Clock

Built from your seal

This one is built out of your seal. The round badge on your vans and your shirts becomes the shape for the whole page, so the hero photo sits inside a big gold ring with RESTORATION and HAWAII running around it, marked off like a 24 hour clock. The buttons are round, the cards have soft corners, and each service sits in a rounded pill with a small photo of the work.

It's the friendliest of the three, and it puts your family right up front. For a company whose mantra is treating every customer as if they were family, that felt like the honest place to start. The navy and gold are the exact colors off your seal.

Type and color

A sturdy rounded font that matches the friendly lettering on your seal, with an easy to read font underneath. Deep navy and your seal's gold, with one full gold band for your mantra.

The trade-off

The seal shapes carry the whole personality, which makes this one feel like nobody but MD. That's a strength as long as the seal stays as it is. It's also the softest of the three, so it may feel more homey than some property managers and adjusters expect.

Worth trying. Reload the page and watch the ring turn into place around the photo, then rest your cursor on the three job cards and the service pills.

Direction two

Turnaround

Before and after

This one leads with your work. The top of the page shows a real MD job, a closet with mold on the walls beside the same closet finished, with a line down the middle you can drag back and forth. When the page loads, the line sweeps across on its own, so the first thing anyone sees is the turnaround.

Every section sits on its own background, so the page reads in clear chunks. How a job runs is a three step timeline with a photo for each step, and each service shows a before and after from your own jobs wherever we have one.

Type and color

One clean, very readable font for everything. White, a light blue gray and navy take turns section by section, with your gold on the buttons and labels.

The trade-off

Nothing sells restoration like a before and after, and this direction puts yours front and center. It also needs good matched pairs, and most of the ones on your current site are older phone photos, so shooting before and after on new jobs would make this one shine.

Worth trying. Reload it and watch the line sweep across the closet, then drag it yourself. On a phone, scroll down and the call bar slides up from the bottom.

Direction three

Fleet

Bold and established

The boldest of the three. Your whole team and fleet photo runs edge to edge with the headline set right across it, and a gold stripe underneath carries your number the same way the stripe runs down the side of your vans. It shows how big and how local you are before anyone reads a word.

Your story sits in a white panel laid over a photo of a crew at work, with your founding year, your team and your fleet in big numbers. The services are one large numbered list instead of boxes or icons.

Type and color

A tall, heavy, all caps font in the spirit of the stripe lettering on your vans, with a plain font underneath. Navy, white and a cool gray, with the gold doing all the shouting.

The trade-off

It's the most confident of the three, and nobody else on Oahu can show a photo like that one. It also leans on that one photo more than anything else, and the copy we have is a small file from your current site, so a fresh full size team shot would make it even stronger.

Worth trying. Reload it and watch the team photo settle while the gold stripe slides in, then move your cursor down the services list.

What's the same in all three

Every direction opens the same way. Your headline, your number and a call button come first, and on a phone the call button stays on screen the whole way down the page. On your current site it disappears after the first screen. Each one then answers the first questions, like how fast you can get there and whether you take their insurance, before it moves through your story, your mantra, how a job runs and your services.

There's also a Text us button next to Call in all three. You mentioned wanting a text option for emergencies, so it's here to show where it could sit on the site. It's only a placeholder.

The photos are the ones already on your site. A new photo session of your crews, your fleet and real jobs would change these pages a lot, in a good way, and the new photos would drop into whichever direction you like.

If you want to take it further

If one of these feels right, it becomes the look for the whole site. The rest of the homepage, your service pages, your testimonials and your careers page would be designed from it, and the real words would be written with you.
