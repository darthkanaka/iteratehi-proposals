The site already ranks well where it counts

A Honolulu search for a child psychiatrist brings up the site near the top of Google.

For the new site The new home page keeps the words families search with, and every old address forwards, so these positions carry over.

2

The Google map is the gap

A map of three practices sat above the results on 25 of 28 care searches, and the clinic wasn't on any of them. OhanaPsych appeared on 17 and BrighterMind Psychiatry on 13.

For the new site Claiming and completing the clinic's Google listing in step three is the biggest single step toward that map.

3

Families search by who and where

On Oahu, searches for a psychiatrist in Honolulu and for ADHD testing each run 100 to 1,000 a month. “Child psychiatrist” with Honolulu or Oahu is the most common way families put it, and TRICARE comes up often.

For the new site The home page names the clinic's care in a sentence or two using those words, and lists every insurance plan as plain text.

4

Three pages cover where visitors already go

Over the last year, 40 percent of visits started on the home page, 20 percent on the providers' pages, 11 percent on Connect and 7 percent on the Team page.

For the new site Home, Our Story and Team Kahala, plus a Connect button on every page, match where 77 percent of visits already start.

5

The current site has loose ends

The site has used 221 addresses since 2016, and only 10 pages can be reached by clicking from the home page. About 67 percent of the Google appearances that weren't for the clinic's name came from pages about services the clinic dropped or keeps off the site.

For the new site Every old address gets a fresh forward to the right new page, and each page gets a title that says who the clinic is and where.
