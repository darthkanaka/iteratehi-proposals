Three directions for the new homepage

Cathy, here are three directions for the top of your new homepage. Each one uses your logo, your photos, and your own section headings, so you are reacting to a real design instead of a description.

These are starting points, not a pick one and we are done. The most common outcome is that you like the feel of one, a section from another, and the color from a third, and we combine them. Tell us what pulls you in and what puts you off, and we build from there.

Read this first

These are design comps, not a working website. Each one stops after the treatment section and jumps to the footer, because the point right now is the look and feel rather than the whole page. The paragraphs are placeholder Latin on purpose, since writing the real words is its own step that happens after a direction is picked. Nothing on these pages clicks. The rest of the homepage, your team, the appointment request flow, and everything else gets designed and built once we know which direction we are working in.

Direction one

Pua

Built out of your own logo

This one is built out of your own mark. The five petals in your logo become the shape language for the whole page, so photos sit inside soft arches, cards have generous rounded corners, and the gold runs as a gradient from light to deep instead of one flat color. The sunset photo sits in an arch with soft petal shapes behind it, and your location, hours, and insurance sit together in one long rounded bar underneath, so the three things people ask first are answered right away. Your mission sits beside the flower from your logo, and its petals drift together into place as you scroll down to it.

Pua means flower, and it is also the word for the children. For a practice named after a flower that takes care of kids, that felt right. This is the warmest of the three and the closest in spirit to what you have now, with shape and depth doing the work your current site asks color to do on its own.

Type and color

The headlines use a geometric rounded sans that echoes the lettering in your own logo, with a warm readable sans underneath. The colors are your cream, sand, and gold, with the gold as a two tone gradient pulled off the mark and a soft sage used sparingly.

The trade-off

The shapes carry the whole personality here, which ties this design closely to your logo. That is a strength while the mark stays as it is. It is also the friendliest of the three, which is right for families and may read as less formal than some of your referring physicians expect.

Worth trying. Watch the arch settle into place when the page loads, then rest your cursor on the three approach cards and the treatment pills.

Direction two

Daybreak

The disciplined one

This is the quiet, disciplined one. It uses warm off white paper, one big confident headline, and small serif captions, so it reads like a considered publication rather than a template. There are no boxes anywhere. Your approach reads like a magazine spread, with the heading on one side and three numbered points on the other, and your treatments sit in an open grid divided by one thin cross. The sunset photo runs the full width of the page as a wide cinematic band right under the headline, with only a small caption on it, and it drifts as you scroll. Your mission sits on a faint pattern of the flower from your logo, and the flowers warm up around your cursor as you move across it.

Your story photo is set in black and white with a small caption under it, the way a magazine would run it. This is the direction that looks most like a practice that knows exactly what it is doing, and it is the one that would hold up best as you add more pages and more content later.

Type and color

One strong sans does all the headline and body work, with a warm serif for the small captions and numbers. The colors are warm paper and near black, with gold saved for the accents, like the buttons, the numbers, and the second line of the headline.

The trade-off

The restraint is the point, and restraint can read as cool. There is no softness in the shapes here, so all the warmth has to come from the photography and the words. That puts more weight on the headshot session than the other two, and it makes the writing pass matter more.

Worth trying. Scroll slowly past the sunset photo and watch it drift, then move your cursor slowly across the mission section.

Direction three

Evening Light

Richer and more established

This is the boldest of the three. The page opens on a dark warm brown with a large serif headline running right across the sunset photo, and a gold bar underneath carrying location, hours, and insurance. Your story sits in a cream panel that overlaps the canoe photo, and your mission gets a full width moment over that same photo instead of being buried further down the page.

The treatment list is worth looking at closely here. It is set in large serif rather than in boxes, and when you rest your cursor on a treatment, the row eases to the right, the name turns gold italic, and a thin gold line draws in underneath. This direction pushes your palette furthest, so it is the best read on whether richer feels right or feels like a different practice.

Type and color

The headlines use a high contrast editorial serif with a plain sans underneath. The colors are deep warm brown and cream, with a brighter gold carrying the accents.

The trade-off

It reads the most established of the three, and it is also the furthest from what you have now. A dark page gives photography real weight, which is a good part of why it feels considered. The photos anchor this design, so it depends most on strong images from the new headshot session.

Worth trying. Reload it and watch the photo settle into place, then move your cursor slowly down the treatment list.

What is the same in all three

Every direction opens the same way and answers the same first questions. The navigation always gives people a way to call you, with a Connect button next to it, and your location, hours, and insurance sit right under the opening headline. Each one then moves through your story, your mission, how you care for families, and what you offer.

The photos are the ones we already have from your current site. The new headshots from the photo session will change these pages quite a bit, in a good way, and they will drop into whichever direction you pick.

There is one thing worth doing rather than reading about. Open each direction and scroll through it instead of only looking at the pictures above, because how a page moves is a good part of what separates these from your current site. Nothing bounces or spins. Sections ease in once as you reach them, the few things that follow your scroll or your cursor move slowly, and if you have Reduce Motion switched on, everything simply appears.

What comes after this

Once you tell us which direction we are working in, that becomes the design system for the whole site. The rest of the homepage gets built out from it, including your team, the appointment request flow, and the mission page. The appointment forms in particular are their own piece of work, since routing a parent to the right form and then making the second one obvious takes real thought and testing rather than a link.

What we need from you

Look at all three on your phone as well as your computer, since most of your patients arrive on a phone. Then tell us which one feels closest, and anything from the other two you want pulled into it. Nothing here is locked.
