Three directions for the new homepage

Cathy, here are three directions for the top of your new homepage. Each one uses your logo, your photos, and your own section headings, so you are reacting to a real design instead of a description.

These are starting points, not a pick one and we are done. The most common outcome is that you like the feel of one, a section from another, and the color from a third, and we combine them. Tell us what pulls you in and what puts you off, and we build from there.

Read this first

These are design comps, not a working website. Each one stops after the treatment section and jumps to the footer, because the point right now is the look and feel rather than the whole page. The paragraphs are placeholder Latin on purpose, since writing the real words is its own step that happens after a direction is picked. Nothing on these pages clicks. The rest of the homepage, your team, the appointment request flow, and everything else gets designed and built once we know which direction we are working in.

Direction one

Sunlit

Closest to the warmth you already have

This one keeps the sand and gold you use today and just builds it properly. A full width sunset photo opens the page, then a white card lifts up over the bottom of it holding your location, hours, and insurance, so the three things people ask first are answered before they scroll. Section backgrounds alternate between cream, white, and sand so each block feels like its own moment instead of one long stack.

If Kimo wants the site to still feel like Kahala Clinic, this is that. It is the same personality, with depth, spacing, and a real typeface doing the work your current site asks color alone to do.

Type and color

A warm serif for headlines and a clean sans for reading. Your existing cream, sand, and gold, with a deep gold band carrying the mission.

Building it in Webflow

Almost all of it is standard Webflow. The layout, the rounded cards, the lifted white card over the photo, and the hover effects are all normal settings. The only custom piece is the small script that fades sections in as you scroll, which is one block of code pasted once in the site settings. Two fonts get switched on in the dashboard.

Direction two

Clear Morning

Lighter, calmer, more contemporary

This is the quiet one. Mostly white and warm off white with a modern sans throughout. The sunset photo runs across the top of the page and off the right edge, and your headline sits on a white card that lifts off it, holding the two buttons and your location, hours, and insurance together in one place. Above the logo there is a thin bar carrying your phone number and hours on one side and a split between new patients and current patients on the other, which is the single most useful thing we saw on the best practice sites we studied.

The approach section becomes numbered rows separated by hairlines instead of boxes, and the four groups you care for get their own band right under the hero. Gold shows up only in small doses, which makes it read as an accent rather than the whole mood.

Type and color

All sans, one family for headlines and one for body. White and warm off white with a deep slate for the darker bands, and gold kept small.

Building it in Webflow

This is the most straightforward of the three to build. Everything is grids, hairlines, and standard hover states. The photo behind the top of the page and the card sitting over it are both normal Webflow positioning. Same one scroll script as the others. Two fonts get switched on in the dashboard.

Direction three

Evening Light

Richer and more established

The boldest of the three. The page opens on a dark warm brown with a large serif headline running right across the sunset photo, and a gold bar underneath carrying location, hours, and insurance. Your story sits in a cream panel that overlaps the canoe photo, and your mission gets a full width moment over that same photo instead of being buried further down the page.

The treatment list is worth looking at closely here, since it is set in large serif rather than in boxes. This direction pushes your palette furthest, so it is the best read on whether richer feels right or feels like a different practice.

Type and color

A high contrast editorial serif with a plain sans underneath. Deep warm brown and cream, with a brighter gold carrying the accents.

Building it in Webflow

All native except two things worth naming. The headline sitting over the photo and the cream panel overlapping the canoe photo are both normal Webflow positioning. The one extra piece is a second version of your logo in cream for the dark sections, which we would make once and load in. Same scroll script, two fonts switched on in the dashboard.

What is the same in all three

Every direction opens the same way and answers the same first questions. Your phone number sits in the navigation with a Connect button next to it, and your location, hours, and insurance are visible before anyone scrolls. Each one then moves through your story, your mission, how you care for families, and what you offer.

The photos are the ones we already have from your current site. The new headshots from the photo session will change these pages quite a bit, in a good way, and they will drop into whichever direction you pick.

One more thing to look at while you scroll. Nothing on these pages bounces or spins. Sections ease in once as you reach them and then stay put, and if a visitor has motion turned off on their phone everything simply appears. That is deliberate for a practice like yours.

What comes after this

Once you tell us which direction we are working in, that becomes the design system for the whole site. The rest of the homepage gets built out from it, including your team, the appointment request flow, and the mission page. The appointment forms in particular are their own piece of work, since routing a parent to the right form and then making the second one obvious takes real thought and testing rather than a link.

What we need from you

Look at all three on your phone as well as your computer, since most of your patients arrive on a phone. Then tell us which one feels closest, and anything from the other two you want pulled into it. Nothing here is locked.
