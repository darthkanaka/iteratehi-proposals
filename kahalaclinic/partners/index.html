A new website for Kahala Clinic

A full rebuild of kahalaclinic.org. For most families the website is the first real impression of the practice, and usually the only one they get before deciding whether to call. The goal is a site that feels the way the clinic does in person: polished and professional, and warm and inviting enough that a family feels welcome before they ever walk through the door.

That comes with a clearer path for new patients, web searchability that protects the position the current site has already earned, and professional photography of the team. $6,500 to $8,000, billed in three steps across about three months.

Prepared for: Dr. Cathy Bell and the partners of Kahala Clinic for Children and Family From: Kawika Lopez, Elevate Media HI
Scope

What we're building

The work is ordered by what it is worth. First, a site that looks like the practice it represents, because a parent comparing two clinics online is judging quality before they have spoken to anyone. Second, a site that moves people to act, which here means booking an appointment or completing the intake forms. Search comes third, and matters most in not losing what the current site has already earned. The three groups below are covered by the fixed price. Photography sits just after them and is billed separately.

First priority Looking like the practice you are What a family concludes about the clinic before they have read a word.

A design built for the clinic

Not a template with the logo swapped in. Sections that vary from one to the next, a consistent set of colors and type, and pages that hold a visitor's attention. The clinic is warm and inviting, and the site should communicate that before someone reads a word of it.

The existing writing, edited down

The clinic's message and voice stay as they are. The work is shortening and tightening it for the new layout, so a visitor gets to the point faster.

Second priority Turning visitors into patients Moving someone from reading the site to booking an appointment or starting the intake forms.

A homepage aimed at one outcome

Almost all of it works toward a single action: requesting an appointment. Everything that does not serve that gets smaller or moves out of the way. A Connect button sits in the navigation on every page, so the step is never more than one click away no matter where someone lands.

A team page that helps a parent choose

Providers and their bios on a single page, with no clicking through to find a person. Families often decide based on something small and specific in a bio, so the page is built to let them find that quickly. It is also built so adding or removing someone does not disturb the rest of the layout, which is true of every section on the new site.

A clearer route to the intake forms

Today a new patient has to choose the right version of Form 1 from three options, then know that Form 2 is also required. The new site asks a question or two, sends them to the correct form, and makes the second step obvious.

The forms themselves stay as they are

They are hosted by Updox and connected to the clinic's records, so they are not ours to redesign and this proposal does not attempt to. Everything leading up to them is what improves.

Third priority Web searchability and launch Keeping the traffic the current site already earns, then building on it.

Carrying the current site's searchability forward

The existing site ranks better than it gets credit for, and protecting that through the move is the most important technical task in the project. Google attaches rankings to specific web addresses, so every old address gets a permanent redirect to its replacement and the clinic keeps the same domain. Expect a few weeks of softer traffic after launch regardless, while Google recrawls the new pages. That is normal and it recovers.

Searchability built into the new pages

Research into what Oahu families actually search for, then pages written and structured around those terms, plus the underlying code that tells Google this is a medical practice in Honolulu and which services it offers.

Google Business Profile and directory listings

The profile claimed and completed, which is what places the clinic in Google's map results. Name, address, and phone corrected across Yelp, Healthgrades, WebMD, Vitals, Apple Maps, and Bing so they agree with each other.

Migration, launch, and retiring the old site

kahalaclinic.org points at the new site, the old one comes down, and former providers stop appearing in search results.

Priced separately

Photography and additional visuals

Professional headshots of the whole team, in one session of about half an hour for everyone, shot outdoors around Kahala rather than against a backdrop. Original photography of real people is a large part of what separates a site that reads as professionally built from one that does not, so it is strongly recommended, but it sits outside the fixed price rather than inside it.

The same line covers any other imagery or graphics the pages turn out to need as they come together. Together these run up to $1,500, and anything unused is not billed.

Timing

Live in about six weeks

From the day work starts, the site is live in roughly 45 days, which is a deliberately conservative estimate. The work on web searchability continues past launch, which is why the payments run across three months while the site itself is up sooner.

One thing to plan around

Three providers are leaving within the next two to six weeks, and the new site lands at around six. There may be a short stretch where the current site still lists someone who has gone. The simplest fix is to remove them from the existing site in the meantime, at no cost. The clinic can do that directly, or send it over and we will handle it.

Not included

One option for later

A short explainer video

An animated piece that walks a visitor through what the clinic does in a minute or two, aimed at someone who would rather watch than read. It suits the practice, and the tools for producing them have become far more accessible than they used to be. It also carries its own script, its own budget, and its own set of decisions, so it is better taken up once the new site is live and you can see what the pages need.
